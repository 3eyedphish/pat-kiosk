0449	Tablet dissolution testing
0450	Automated drug-release tests with immersion probes, without sampling.
0451	Dissolution tests show how fast and how completely a tablet releases its active ingredient. Optical immersion probes measure the release directly in the test vessel.
0452	The task
0453	The pharmacopoeias (EP, USP, JP) prescribe the procedure: six tablets, several test intervals. Manual sampling, filtering and photometry are labor-intensive and prone to error, and new dosage forms keep adding samples.
0454	Measured in the vessel
0455	Probes connected to a spectrometer by optical fibers sit in the paddle stirrer next to the dissolving tablet. As the active ingredient dissolves, its absorption rises until the tablet has fully dissolved and the curve levels off. With several vessels, a multiplexer connects all probes to one spectrometer.
0456	Faster with robotics
0457	A robotic arm can move the probe from vessel to vessel, rinsing and drying it in between, for very short measurement intervals.
0458	Which probe
0459	The Excalibur Lab, with its monolithic quartz measuring head, has proven itself here. For narrow vessels with small lids, the slim Falcata Lab with interchangeable path-length tips fits better.

0467	Spray drying: residual moisture in an API
0468	NIR through a sight glass replaces Karl Fischer sampling.
0469	Spray drying turns a solution of active ingredient and excipients into powder, in less than a second per droplet. The residual moisture of that powder has to stay within tight limits.
0470	Why moisture matters
0471	Too much residual moisture makes the powder clump and hurts flowability, yield and ultimately the drug's performance. Particle size and morphology, which shape the release profile, are set in the same step.
0472	The old way
0473	Karl Fischer titration needs samples taken and analyzed by hand in the lab. By the time a result comes back, the dryer has moved on.
0474	Measured without contact
